Store replenishment · Software engineering / Case study

StoreLoop

Compare three ways to plan a delivery run from one distribution centre to a group of retail stores.

  • JavaScript
  • Combinatorial optimisation
  • Dynamic programming
Open interactive demoDownload source

The dispatch decision

The load and store list are fixed. The dispatcher chooses the order of visits for one vehicle that must return to its starting depot. The demo lets you select a network, change its size and inspect each algorithm’s stop sequence.

Three ways to build a route

Nearest neighbour always chooses the closest unvisited store. A 2-opt search then tries reversing sections of that route to shorten it. A Held–Karp dynamic programme independently finds the shortest possible tour under the model, providing a reference for evaluating both heuristics.

What the benchmark found

Across 100 seeded nine-store networks, 2-opt reduced total modelled distance by 7.8% against nearest neighbour. It matched the exact optimum in 78 networks. Its total distance across all runs was 0.72% above the exact total.

How to check the result

The downloadable benchmark verifies that each route visits every store once and starts and ends at the distribution centre. On ten smaller networks it compares the exact solver against exhaustive enumeration. The exact solver stores a result for each subset and final stop, so its resource requirements grow exponentially with the number of stores.

Where the model ends

These are synthetic locations with straight-line distances. A retail trial would need road travel times, delivery windows, driver hours and vehicle capacity. The reported change is a simulation result and cannot establish real mileage or cost savings.
